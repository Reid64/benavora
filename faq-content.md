### PAGE 1 — REAL ESTATE AGENTS

**Q1: How does the age of a roof affect a buyer's ability to get homeowners insurance?**
Insurers weigh roof age heavily; many carriers move older roofs from full replacement-cost coverage to actual cash value, or require an inspection confirming remaining useful life, once a roof passes a certain age. Because a mortgage typically requires the buyer to secure insurance, an aging or damaged roof can jeopardize both insurability and loan approval. We provide dated, documented roof-condition reports agents can share with buyers, lenders, and carriers to keep a deal moving.

**Q2: Can you provide a documented roof inspection report fast enough for a contract timeline?**
Yes. We prioritize inspections tied to active real estate transactions and deliver a written report with photos documenting the roof's condition, apparent age, and any deficiencies. This gives agents third-party documentation to support negotiations, option-period decisions, or insurance underwriting without stalling the closing.

**Q3: What is an escrow holdback for roof repairs, and when is it used?**
An escrow holdback sets aside a portion of the seller's proceeds at closing to pay for roof repairs that cannot be completed before the closing date — often due to weather or contractor scheduling. The lender must approve the arrangement, and funds are released once the work is completed and verified (a common industry standard is holding roughly 1.5x the contractor's estimate). We can provide the written scope and estimate that lenders and title companies require to set up the holdback.

**Q4: Should a seller replace the roof before listing, or offer a credit instead?**
It depends on the roof's remaining life and the local buyer pool. If the roof has several years of life left, documentation and minor repairs are usually the better financial move; if it is at or near end-of-life or failing an insurance inspection, replacement (or a negotiated credit/holdback) may be necessary to keep the home financeable. We give sellers an honest assessment so the decision is based on condition, not guesswork.

**Q5: What should buyers know about roof age when making an offer?**
Buyers should treat roof age as a financing and insurance issue, not just a future repair cost — an older roof can raise premiums (which affect debt-to-income calculations) or trigger a coverage denial. We recommend a documented roof inspection during the option period so buyers understand remaining life, likely near-term costs, and any insurance red flags before they are locked in.

### PAGE 4 — INVESTORS

**Q1: How fast can you turn around a roof for a fix-and-flip?**
We prioritize investor timelines and can typically complete a standard residential tear-off and re-roof in a short window once materials are staged, so roofing does not become the bottleneck on your flip. Fast, documented completion helps you hit listing dates and supports a higher resale price.

**Q2: Should I repair or replace the roof on an investment property?**
For a rental you plan to hold, targeted repairs often make sense if the roof is otherwise serviceable and the damage is isolated; for a flip, a new roof can remove a buyer objection and support the sale price. Over-improving is an ROI killer — the deciding factors are the roof's age, the scope of damage, and your exit strategy. We give you a straight repair-vs-replace recommendation with costs.

**Q3: Can you provide a fast estimate for underwriting or deal analysis?**
Yes. We can provide prompt written estimates and condition assessments you can plug into your rehab budget or ARV (after-repair value) analysis before you close. Accurate roof numbers early help you avoid overbidding or discovering a major line item after purchase.

**Q4: What roofing materials give the best durability for rental properties?**
For most Central Texas rentals, quality architectural asphalt shingles offer the best balance of cost, durability, and hail performance, and impact-resistant (Class 4) shingles can further reduce storm-related repair frequency. We help investors weigh upfront cost against expected repair frequency and tenant-turnover downtime rather than defaulting to the cheapest option.

**Q5: Is a roof replacement a repair or a capital improvement for my taxes?**
A full roof replacement is generally treated as a capital improvement (depreciated over the property's schedule, commonly 27.5 years for residential rentals) rather than a currently-deductible repair, while isolated fixes are more often repairs — but classification depends on the specifics, so confirm with your tax professional. We provide clear invoices and scope documentation to support whichever treatment your accountant applies.

### PAGE 6 — COMMERCIAL PROPERTY OWNERS

**Q1: What are the best flat or low-slope roofing systems for a commercial building?**
The most common low-slope systems are TPO, EPDM, and modified bitumen. TPO is a single-ply membrane with heat-welded seams and a reflective white surface that can help with cooling costs; EPDM is a proven rubber membrane with a long track record; modified bitumen offers strong puncture resistance for high-traffic roofs. We assess your building's drainage, rooftop traffic, and budget to recommend the right system rather than a one-size-fits-all answer.

**Q2: How long does a commercial flat roof last?**
With proper installation and maintenance, TPO and EPDM systems generally last in the range of 20-30 years and modified bitumen around 15-20 years, though installation quality, membrane thickness, and maintenance drive the actual result more than material choice alone. Twice-yearly inspections and prompt repairs are the biggest levers on reaching full service life.

**Q3: How do you minimize disruption to my business during roofing work?**
We plan staging, access, and the noisiest work around your operating hours and coordinate with you on parking, deliveries, and tenant/customer traffic. For occupied buildings we phase the work and keep clear communication so the business stays open and productive throughout.

**Q4: Do you offer a preventive maintenance program for commercial roofs?**
Yes. A structured program of scheduled inspections, drain and debris cleaning, seam and flashing checks, and documented repairs catches small deficiencies before they become interior-damage events. Proactive maintenance meaningfully extends roof life, and documented maintenance history increasingly matters for commercial insurance, since some carriers scrutinize inspection records when evaluating storm-damage claims.

**Q5: Will insurance cover storm or hail damage to my commercial roof?**
Storm and hail damage are commonly covered perils, but commercial policies vary and may carry percentage wind/hail deductibles or cosmetic-damage limitations. We provide detailed, documented damage assessments that support your claim and can work with your adjuster; keeping current inspection records strengthens your position if a claim is disputed.

**Q6: How do I decide whether to repair or replace an aging commercial roof?**
Track your repair spending: if it is trending sharply upward or a single leak threatens inventory, equipment, or tenant operations, replacement often delivers better long-term ROI than continued patching. We provide condition ratings and remaining-life estimates so you can weigh repair costs, business-interruption risk, and insurance implications with real numbers.

### PAGE 7 — ROOF INSTALLATION

**Q1: What is the difference between a tear-off and an overlay (re-roof over existing shingles)?**
A tear-off removes all existing roofing down to the deck, allowing inspection and repair of the decking before new materials go on; an overlay installs new shingles over the existing layer, which is faster and cheaper but hides the deck and adds weight. Building codes generally limit a roof to two layers, and overlays typically do not last as long. We usually recommend a tear-off for the longest-lasting result and to catch hidden deck damage.

**Q2: How long does a new roof installation take?**
A typical single-family asphalt-shingle roof is often completed in one to two days, though larger, steeper, or more complex roofs and tile or metal systems take longer. Weather, roof size, and the number of existing layers being removed all affect the timeline — we give you a specific estimate before we start.

**Q3: What shingle types and options should I consider?**
Most homes use asphalt shingles, which range from basic three-tab to thicker architectural (dimensional) shingles, plus impact-resistant (Class 4) options that perform better against hail. In Central Texas, impact-resistant architectural shingles are a popular upgrade because of frequent hail, and some carriers offer premium credits for them. We walk you through appearance, durability, and cost trade-offs for your home.

**Q4: What is involved in a full roof replacement?**
A full replacement generally includes removing the old roofing, inspecting and repairing the decking as needed, installing new underlayment and flashing, and installing the new roof system, followed by cleanup and a final inspection. Exposing the deck is a key advantage because it lets us find and fix rot or damaged sheathing that an overlay would conceal.

**Q5: How much does a new roof cost?**
Cost depends on roof size, pitch, material, the number of existing layers to remove, and any decking repairs, so a written estimate after inspection is the only accurate figure. We provide a detailed, itemized estimate up front and never ask for full payment before the work is complete.

### PAGE 8 — ROOF REPAIRS

**Q1: Should I repair or replace my roof?**
Repair is usually the right call when the roof is relatively young and the damage is isolated. Replacement makes more sense when the roof is old, damage is widespread, or you have already had several repairs, since repeated patching on a worn roof can add up to the cost of replacement anyway. We give you an honest assessment based on age, scope, and condition.

**Q2: Where do most roof leaks actually come from?**
The majority of roof leaks start at penetrations and transitions — flashing around chimneys, vents, skylights, and valleys, and failed pipe boots — rather than in the open field of shingles. That is why a leak does not automatically mean you need a new roof; a targeted repair to the flashing or boot often solves it. We diagnose the true source rather than chasing the stain.

**Q3: Can a roof leak be repaired, or does the whole roof need replacing?**
Many leaks are caused by localized issues and can be fixed without replacing the roof, especially when the surrounding shingles are still in good shape. If the leak has caused decking damage or is widespread, or the roof is near end-of-life, replacement may be the better long-term option. We start with an accurate diagnosis before recommending scope.

**Q4: Will homeowners insurance cover my roof repair?**
Sudden, accidental damage from a covered peril such as wind or hail is commonly covered, while gradual wear, age, and lack of maintenance generally are not. In Texas, remember that wind/hail claims often fall under a separate percentage deductible, so a smaller repair may cost less than your deductible. We document the damage clearly so you can make an informed decision about filing.

**Q5: How soon should I fix a small roof leak?**
Promptly — even a minor leak can travel along framing and lead to mold, insulation damage, and drywall repairs that cost far more than the original fix. If a permanent repair cannot happen immediately, temporary measures like tarping protect the interior in the meantime. Early action is almost always the cheaper path.

### PAGE 9 — EMERGENCY ROOF TARPING

**Q1: How fast can you tarp my roof after storm damage?**
We treat active or imminent water intrusion as a priority and respond quickly to get a protective tarp in place, especially when rain is in the forecast. Fast tarping stops interior water damage from compounding while a permanent repair is scheduled.

**Q2: Does insurance cover emergency roof tarping?**
In most cases, yes — when the underlying damage is from a covered peril such as wind, hail, or a fallen limb, tarping is generally reimbursable because policies require you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage. Keep receipts and photograph the original damage before the tarp goes up so the adjuster can see it. In Texas, your wind/hail deductible still applies to the overall claim.

**Q3: How long can a roof tarp stay on?**
A properly installed heavy-duty tarp is a temporary "dry-in" measure, generally holding up for roughly 30-90 days depending on weather, sun, and installation quality. It should be treated as a stopgap, not a permanent fix — permanent repairs should be scheduled as soon as practical.

**Q4: What happens after tarping and before the permanent repair?**
The tarp buys you a clear window to document the damage, file your insurance claim, get an inspection, and schedule permanent repairs without being rushed into a decision. We coordinate the transition from emergency tarp to permanent work.

**Q5: Should I tarp my own roof or call a professional?**
For steep roofs, large damaged areas, or anyone without proper safety equipment and experience, professional tarping is strongly recommended — a damaged roof is dangerous, and an improperly installed tarp can channel water into the home or blow off. Professional installation also satisfies insurers' documentation expectations and protects your claim.

### PAGE 10 — COMMERCIAL ROOFING

**Q1: What is the best material for a flat or low-slope commercial roof?**
The leading single-ply and low-slope options are TPO, EPDM, and modified bitumen. TPO's heat-welded seams and reflective surface suit energy-conscious buildings; EPDM has a decades-long track record and good cold-weather flexibility; modified bitumen resists punctures on high-traffic roofs. The best choice depends on your building's drainage, rooftop traffic, and budget, which we evaluate on-site.

**Q2: What is the difference between TPO and EPDM?**
Both are proven low-slope membranes that can last decades, but they differ in a few ways: TPO is typically a reflective white membrane with heat-welded seams, while EPDM is a black rubber membrane usually joined with tape or adhesive seams. TPO's reflectivity can lower cooling costs in hot climates like Central Texas, while EPDM has the longer historical track record. We help you weigh energy performance, seam durability, and cost.

**Q3: How long does a commercial roof last, and how do I maximize it?**
Expected service life is roughly 20-30 years for TPO and EPDM and about 15-20 years for modified bitumen, but installation quality and maintenance matter more than the material label. Twice-yearly inspections, keeping drains clear, and repairing seams and flashing promptly are the most effective ways to reach the roof's design life.

**Q4: Do you offer commercial roof maintenance and inspection programs?**
Yes. A scheduled program of inspections, drain and debris cleaning, and documented minor repairs prevents small issues from becoming costly interior-damage events and helps keep warranties valid. Documented maintenance history is also increasingly important for insurance, since carriers may scrutinize inspection records after a storm claim.

**Q5: What causes ponding water on a flat roof, and is it a problem?**
Ponding — water that remains on the roof more than a day or two after rain — usually points to drainage or slope issues and can stress the membrane and shorten its life over time. Persistent ponding should be evaluated, because ongoing standing water is a common driver of low-slope roof deterioration and failure. We assess drainage as part of any commercial inspection.

### PAGE 11 — TILE ROOFING

**Q1: Do I need to reinforce my roof structure for tile?**
Possibly. Tile is far heavier than asphalt shingles — concrete tile in particular weighs several times more than shingles per square — so a home not originally engineered for tile may need a structural evaluation before installation. We arrange a structural assessment when converting from a lighter material so the framing can safely carry the load.

**Q2: How long does a tile roof last?**
Clay tile is among the longest-lasting roofing materials, frequently lasting well over 50 years and often 100+, while concrete tile commonly lasts around 50 years. Importantly, the underlayment beneath the tile has a shorter life than the tile itself and typically needs replacement decades before the tiles do, even when the tiles remain sound.

**Q3: What is the difference between clay and concrete tile?**
Clay tile is kiln-fired, extremely color-stable, lighter than concrete, and generally longer-lasting, but costs more; concrete tile is more budget-friendly and offers more shape and color options but is heavier and absorbs more water, which can encourage algae or moss in damp areas. We help you weigh weight, longevity, appearance, and budget for your specific home.

**Q4: Can you replace just a few cracked or broken tiles instead of the whole roof?**
Yes. Individual damaged tiles can usually be replaced without redoing the entire roof, which is one of tile's advantages. However, if water has reached and damaged the underlayment or decking, a larger repair or re-underlayment may be needed even when most tiles are reusable. We diagnose whether the issue is surface-level or underneath.

**Q5: Is a tile roof worth the higher cost?**
Tile costs more up front than asphalt but can last two to four times longer and stands up well to heat and sun, so its longer lifespan can offset the higher initial price over the life of the home. We provide an itemized estimate so you can compare lifetime value, not just the day-one price.

### PAGE 12 — GUTTERS

**Q1: What is the difference between seamless and sectional gutters?**
Seamless gutters are formed on-site from one continuous piece of metal, so the only joints are at corners and downspouts, which means fewer leak points and less debris buildup. Sectional gutters are joined from shorter pieces and cost less up front, but each seam is a potential leak point that can loosen over time — a process accelerated by Central Texas heat.

**Q2: What gutter size do I need in Central Texas?**
Because Central Texas storms can drop heavy rain quickly, larger 6-inch gutters are a common recommendation to handle high-volume flow, though the right size depends on your roof's drainage area and pitch. We calculate the drainage area and confirm whether additional downspouts are needed so water is carried away efficiently.

**Q3: Are gutter guards worth it?**
Gutter guards can significantly reduce how often gutters need cleaning and help prevent clogs, but they do not eliminate maintenance entirely and their value depends on your roof type and nearby trees. Homes under heavy oak or other tree canopy benefit most. We assess whether guards make sense for your specific home rather than selling them by default.

**Q4: How often should gutters be cleaned?**
At least twice a year — typically spring and fall — is the general guideline, with more frequent cleaning for homes surrounded by trees that shed leaves, seed pods, and needles. Even seamless gutters and guarded systems need periodic checks to clear debris and confirm downspouts flow freely.

**Q5: Do I really need gutters in Central Texas?**
Yes — the region's heavy, fast downpours make gutters important for directing water away from your foundation, siding, and landscaping, which helps prevent erosion and foundation issues common to Central Texas soils. We help you decide on placement and downspout routing for effective drainage.

### PAGE 13 — HISTORICAL HAIL STORMS (Hail History Lookup Tool)

**Q1: How can I check if my property has been hit by hail?**
You can look up the hail history for a specific address using databases built on NOAA/National Weather Service storm reports and radar, which show the dates and reported sizes of past hail events near a location. Our address-search tool draws on this official storm-report data so you can see documented hail activity in your area. For an actual damage determination, pair that history with a professional roof inspection.

**Q2: How big does hail need to be to damage a roof?**
Hail around 1 inch in diameter — about the size of a quarter — is the widely recognized threshold where functional damage to standard asphalt shingles begins; the National Weather Service raised its official severe-hail criterion to 1.00 inch in 2010 because significant damage generally does not occur below that size. Impact-resistant (Class 3-4) shingles can withstand larger hail before sustaining damage, and roof age matters too, since older shingles can be damaged by smaller stones.

**Q3: How is hail damage on a roof assessed?**
A trained inspector examines the roof for functional damage — bruising, cracked shingles, and granule loss that exposes the asphalt mat — rather than relying on hail size alone. Insurance adjusters commonly evaluate the number of impact marks within a 10-foot by 10-foot test square to decide between repair and full replacement. We document our findings in writing with photos so you have an independent record before the adjuster arrives.

**Q4: How much time do I have to file a hail damage claim in Texas?**
In Texas you generally have two years to bring a lawsuit over a property insurance claim, and a 2017 law (HB 1774, codified at Texas Insurance Code Section 542A.003) requires you to give your insurer at least 61 days' written notice before filing suit. Separately, your own policy may require you to report the claim much sooner, so file promptly and check your policy's deadlines. We recommend documenting damage and contacting your insurer as soon as it is safe after a storm.

**Q5: Does my roof's age affect how much insurance pays for hail damage?**
Yes. If your policy pays replacement cost value (RCV), it generally pays the full cost to replace the roof minus your deductible; if it pays actual cash value (ACV), it deducts depreciation for the roof's age, so older roofs receive substantially less. Texas policies also commonly apply a separate percentage wind/hail deductible (often 1-2% of your dwelling coverage) rather than a flat dollar amount. Knowing which type of coverage you carry before a storm helps you avoid surprises.

**Q6: Is hail damage always visible from the ground?**
No — hail damage is often not obvious from the ground and may not cause an immediate leak, sometimes taking months or years to show as a problem. That is why an inspection after a significant hail event is worthwhile even if the roof looks fine, particularly on older shingles. Checking documented storm history for your address helps you decide whether an inspection is warranted.
