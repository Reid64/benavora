Human services organizations rely on a layered mix of funding: federal programs through the Department of Health and Human Services and the Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration, state-level funding administered through a state department of human services or equivalent agency, and local foundations that often supplement federal awards or fund gaps federal programs do not cover.

Federal HHS and SAMHSA notices tend to carry detailed compliance and reporting requirements, frequently spelled out in a linked appendix rather than the headline notice text. Funding Intelligence reads the full notice package, including those appendices, and reports every eligibility and compliance requirement it finds, so an organization is not surprised by a data-reporting obligation or a target-population definition after the application is already underway.

State DHS funding is a category that national grant search tools frequently miss, because it is announced through state-specific portals rather than a centralized federal listing service. Opportunity Discovery's source connectors include state agency sources, so a state DHS notice appears in the same feed as a federal HHS listing, rather than requiring a separate manual check of the state agency's own site.

Local foundations often play a specific role for human services organizations: filling a gap a federal award does not cover, or providing bridge funding while a federal application is pending. The Pipeline and CRM system tracks these opportunities against the same program area as the federal award they relate to, so staff can see the full funding picture for a given program rather than treating each source as an isolated application.

Analytics reports win rate and dollars awarded broken out by funder type, which is useful specifically in human services, where comparing federal award performance against state and local foundation performance can inform where to invest limited grant-writing staff time going forward.
