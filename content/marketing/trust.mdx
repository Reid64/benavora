Trust in an automated grant platform comes down to a small number of concrete questions: what does the system do without asking, what record exists of what it did, and where does its information come from. This page answers those questions directly.

No agent in this platform submits an application, sends an outreach message, or commits an organization's identity without a defined human approval step. Opportunity Discovery surfaces candidates; a staff member selects which move forward. Funding Intelligence reports an eligibility gap; a staff member decides whether it is disqualifying. The AI Grant Writer produces a draft; a staff member edits and approves it before it can be used elsewhere in the platform. AutoApply fills a portal form and stops at a review hold; a staff member must approve the completed form before it submits. This sequence is fixed in how the platform is built, not an optional setting.

Every submission carries a record: a timestamp, the staff member who approved it, and a copy of the final submitted form. This exists so that an organization can answer, months or years later, exactly what was submitted and who signed off on it, without relying on memory or a search through old email.

Every factual claim in a drafted narrative carries a citation back to the specific knowledge base entry it came from. If a fact about the organization is not recorded in its own knowledge base, the draft does not assert it. This means a reviewer can verify any claim in a draft against its source the same way they would check a citation in a research document, rather than trusting generated text on faith.
