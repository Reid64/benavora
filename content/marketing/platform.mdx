Benavora is built as six connected systems rather than one monolithic tool. Each one does a narrow job well, and each one hands its output to the next system in the chain: discovery finds a candidate opportunity, funding intelligence reads the notice and scores your fit, the grant writer drafts from your organization's own knowledge base, AutoApply fills the portal fields under a human review gate, and the pipeline and CRM system tracks the result through award or decline. Analytics sits underneath all five, recording what actually happened so the next cycle starts from evidence instead of guesswork.

This separation matters because grant work is not one task. A program officer at a small nonprofit spends time reading notices of funding opportunity that turn out to be a poor fit, re-typing the same organizational facts into a dozen different portals, and losing track of which funder asked for a report by which date. Benavora's six systems exist to remove the repetitive parts of that work while keeping a named person in control of anything that submits, sends, or commits the organization.

Nothing in this platform acts without a defined human checkpoint. Discovery surfaces candidates; a staff member decides which to pursue. The grant writer produces a draft; a staff member edits and approves it before it is used. AutoApply fills a portal; a staff member reviews the filled form before submission is confirmed. This is a deliberate design choice, not a limitation: an autonomous agent that submits under your organization's identity without review is a liability, not a feature.

Each system below has its own page describing what it reads, what it produces, and where the human control point sits. Explore them in the order that matches your current bottleneck, whether that is not finding enough opportunities, not knowing which ones you qualify for, spending too long on first drafts, spending too long on portal data entry, or losing visibility into where every application currently stands.
