AutoApply addresses the least interesting and most repetitive part of grant work: re-entering the same organizational facts into a different portal's field structure for every single application. Once a draft has been approved through the AI Grant Writer and the organization's profile data is current, AutoApply maps that content to the specific fields a target portal requires, including attachments such as budgets and letters of support in the format and location the portal specifies.

The system is explicitly built around a review hold, not around unattended submission. After AutoApply fills a form, it stops. The completed form is presented field by field, exactly as it will appear to the portal, for a staff member to review before anything is sent. This is not a configurable option that can be turned off; it is how the system is built, because an application submitted under an organization's identity without a person confirming it first is a risk no automation should carry on its own.

Not every field can be filled with confidence. Some portal fields ask for a judgment call, such as a certification statement or a discretionary narrative choice, that the system is not positioned to make. Those fields are left blank and flagged clearly, rather than filled with a plausible-looking guess, so staff know exactly what still needs their direct attention before the form is complete.

Once a staff member reviews the held form and approves it, the submission proceeds and the system records a timestamp, the approving staff member, and a copy of the final submitted form. This record exists so that months later, when a program officer asks what exactly was submitted and who approved it, the organization has an exact answer rather than a recollection.

AutoApply's value is time saved on data entry, not judgment removed from the process. The organization's staff remain the ones deciding what gets submitted and when.
