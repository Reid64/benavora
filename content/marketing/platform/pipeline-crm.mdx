The Pipeline and CRM system is where every opportunity lives after it leaves discovery, regardless of what stage it is currently in. A prospect under review, a draft in progress, a submitted application awaiting a decision, an awarded grant with reporting obligations, and a declined application kept for reference all sit in the same structured pipeline, each with a defined stage and an assigned owner.

This solves a specific, common failure mode: application status that lives only in one staff member's inbox or personal spreadsheet, invisible to the rest of the team and vulnerable to being lost entirely if that person is unavailable. With a shared pipeline, any staff member can see what stage a given application is in, who owns it, and what the next required action is, without needing to ask.

Deadlines are tracked across the entire active pipeline in a single view, rather than one portal or one spreadsheet tab at a time. This includes both application deadlines and post-award reporting deadlines, which are tracked against the original application record rather than as a separate, easily forgotten task. A grant that is awarded does not disappear from view; it moves to a new stage with its own reporting obligations attached.

Funder relationship history accumulates over time in the same system. Prior submissions, prior outcomes, and prior communications with a given funder are attached to that funder's record, so staff preparing a new application to a funder the organization has worked with before can see that history without searching through old files.

The pipeline does not move opportunities between stages on its own, and it does not decide when a task is complete. Staff set stages, assign tasks, and confirm reporting obligations directly. The system's role is to make that information visible and organized in one place, not to make decisions on the organization's behalf.
