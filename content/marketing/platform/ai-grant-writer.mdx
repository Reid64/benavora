The AI Grant Writer exists to remove the blank-page problem, not to remove the writer. Every draft it produces starts from the organization's own stored knowledge base: program descriptions, past outcomes, staff qualifications, and organizational history that staff have already recorded, rather than generic language invented for the occasion.

Each factual sentence in a generated draft carries a citation pointing back to the specific knowledge base entry it came from. This is a deliberate constraint: if a claim about the organization cannot be traced to a source document, it does not appear in the draft as a statement of fact. A reviewer checking the draft can click through to the source and confirm it says what the draft claims, the same way they would check a citation in any research document.

Drafts are structured to match the specific notice being applied to, using the section headings, word limits, and required elements stated in that notice rather than a generic template. A notice that asks for a two-page statement of need followed by a one-page evaluation plan produces a draft with those two sections at those lengths, not a five-section template that has to be manually restructured afterward.

Staff can request alternate phrasings of a given section to compare approaches before settling on one, and the system keeps a record of what it drafted versus what staff subsequently edited, so a reviewer coming back to a draft later can see exactly what changed and why.

No draft produced here is submitted anywhere automatically. It exists as an editable document until a staff member reviews it, corrects anything that needs correcting, verifies the citations, and approves it. Only an approved draft becomes eligible to move into AutoApply for portal submission, and that step carries its own separate human review gate.
