# Tarritrix Client Intelligence Intake — Master Document

**Document version:** 1.0
**Effective date:** 2026-05-20
**Owner:** Operator
**Audience:** Operator (during sales calls and onboarding), VA (during data verification), Client (via Asset Hub self-service portal)
**Repository path:** `docs/onboarding/client-intelligence-intake.md`

---

## Purpose

This document defines the complete data set Tarritrix collects from every client during onboarding. The data feeds every agent in the platform, every backlink and citation campaign, every page generation, and every reporting workflow. Incomplete data degrades platform output; this intake is the single instrument that ensures completeness.

This document serves three audiences and three workflows:

1. **Sales calls** — Operator references this document to ask the right questions during discovery, demo, and contract closing. Not all questions are answered on the call; the goal is to surface what the client knows immediately versus what requires document collection later.
2. **Onboarding sessions** — Operator works through each section with the client over a structured onboarding call (recommended 60-90 minutes) or sequence of calls. Data is captured directly into the Tarritrix platform.
3. **Asynchronous self-service** — Client uses the Asset Hub (client portal feature) to upload documents, fill structured forms, and complete sections that require document gathering or internal coordination.

## Section completion expectations

Not every section is fully complete on day one. The platform uses a three-stage evidence unlock model:

- **Tier 1 (Foundational, target: complete within 7 days of onboarding)** — Identity, services, geography, primary credentials, basic brand assets. This unlocks initial page generation and Tier 1 directory citations.
- **Tier 2 (Authority, target: complete within 30 days)** — Insurance carrier relationships, manufacturer certifications, trade affiliations, existing digital footprint, case studies. This unlocks expanded page generation and Tier 2 outreach campaigns.
- **Tier 3 (Dominance, target: complete within 90 days)** — Press history, community involvement, original content assets, full backlink inventory. This unlocks Tier 3 PR campaigns and the full Storm Intelligence Engine page volume.

Each section below indicates its tier assignment, the agents and workflows it feeds, and what happens if the section remains incomplete past its target window.

## Section 1: Business Identity

**Tier:** 1 (Foundational)
**Feeds:** A-01 Intake Processor, A-03 Schema Generator (LocalBusiness JSON-LD), all directory registrations (A-46), all pages generated by A-02, TCPA compliance system, billing system
**Critical for:** Page generation accuracy, Google verification, legal compliance, all directory citations

### Questions

1. **Legal business name** (exact as registered with Secretary of State)
2. **Doing-business-as (DBA) name** if different from legal name
3. **Business entity type** (LLC, S-Corp, C-Corp, Sole Proprietorship, Partnership)
4. **State of incorporation / formation**
5. **Federal Employer Identification Number (EIN)** — required for some directory registrations and partnership applications
6. **State business license number(s)** — including any specialty licenses for roofing, restoration, contracting
7. **Year founded** (used for "X years serving [city]" trust signals on pages)
8. **Number of employees** (used for "team of X technicians" trust signals)
9. **Primary business phone** — the canonical number that appears on every page, every directory, every citation
10. **Business email** (general inquiries) — used for press outreach destinations
11. **Owner / principal name** — used for "About Us" content, manufacturer partner pages, owner bio
12. **Owner email** — used for owner-direct communications, manufacturer certifications
13. **Owner phone (direct)** — used for owner-direct outreach scenarios, manufacturer partner programs
14. **Primary physical business address** (street, city, state, ZIP) — the canonical address for NAP consistency across all citations
15. **Hours of operation** — including emergency/after-hours availability
16. **After-hours emergency phone** if different from primary

### Why this matters

Every directory citation requires consistent NAP (Name, Address, Phone) data. NAP inconsistency across the web is one of the strongest negative signals for local SEO. If the client uses "ABC Roofing LLC" on Yelp, "ABC Roofing" on Google Business Profile, and "ABC Roofing & Restoration LLC" on BBB, Google penalizes the entire citation network. This section establishes the canonical version of every identity element.

### What happens if incomplete

If legal name or address is missing, no directory registrations can begin. Pages can be generated with placeholder data but cannot be published. Tier 1 unlock blocked.

## Section 2: Service Portfolio

**Tier:** 1 (Foundational)
**Feeds:** A-01 Intake Processor (services table), A-02 Page Generator (every service-area, service-city, and service-hub page), A-03 Schema Generator (Service schema), Storm Intelligence Engine targeting, A-46 Directory Registration (business category selection)
**Critical for:** Page generation scope, keyword targeting, service area page architecture

### Questions

1. **Primary services offered** — list each as a discrete service, not bundled (e.g., "roof replacement, roof repair, hail damage restoration, emergency tarping, gutter installation, gutter repair" not "roofing services")
2. **Which services are storm-driven** (eligible for storm-reactive page generation under Authority and Dominance tiers)
3. **Which services are insurance-claim-driven** (eligible for Xactimate rewrite workflows)
4. **Which services are seasonal** and what months are peak demand (e.g., "ice dam removal: December-March")
5. **Which services are highest-margin / highest-priority for lead generation** (used to weight conversion form prominence on pages)
6. **Which services are lowest-margin or loss-leaders** (still offered but not the primary marketing focus)
7. **Any services explicitly NOT offered** that customers might assume (e.g., "we do residential roofing only, not commercial" — used as exclusion language to prevent unqualified leads)
8. **Service-specific pricing models** (flat-rate, time-and-materials, insurance-billed, free-estimate, etc.) — used to set client expectations on pages
9. **Service warranty terms per service** (e.g., "10-year workmanship warranty on roof replacement, 1-year on repairs") — used as trust signals
10. **Service-specific certifications required** (e.g., "GAF certification required for our roof replacements") — links to Section 5 manufacturer partnerships

### Why this matters

A-02 generates one page per service per city per intent. If the client says "we do roofing" without breaking it down, the platform generates generic pages. If the client breaks it into 8 discrete services, the platform generates 8× more pages with 8× more keyword coverage. The granularity here directly determines page volume and ranking surface.

Service hub pages (the most important page type architecturally) are generated one per service. Without a clean service list, hub pages cannot be created.

### What happens if incomplete

Page generation falls back to generic service language ("roofing services"). Service hub pages cannot be generated. Keyword targeting is shallow.

## Section 3: Geographic Coverage

**Tier:** 1 (Foundational)
**Feeds:** A-01 Intake Processor (cities table assignment), A-02 Page Generator (service-area and service-city pages), Storm Intelligence Engine (NOAA polygon filtering), A-04 Map Embed Generator, A-46 Directory Registration (local citation targeting)
**Critical for:** Page volume, storm intelligence accuracy, service area defensibility

### Questions

1. **Primary metro area** (DMA / Designated Market Area — used for storm intelligence radius and Tier 2 backlink targeting)
2. **Primary city** where the business is headquartered
3. **All cities and ZIP codes actively serviced** — list every city, no matter how small. Each becomes a potential page target.
4. **Expansion target cities** — cities the client wants to grow into within 12 months (these get pages but lower priority in publishing cadence)
5. **Service radius from primary location** (used to validate service area claims and prevent overreaching to unrealistic distances)
6. **Cities explicitly NOT serviced** (used as exclusions to prevent unqualified lead capture)
7. **Storm corridors and microclimates within the service area** — operator-defined polygons for areas with distinct weather patterns (e.g., "Tornado Alley corridor through Cleburne County," "Hailstorm impact zone along I-35 corridor"). Used by Storm Intelligence Engine for hyperlocal targeting.
8. **Local landmarks, neighborhoods, and notable areas** within primary cities — used in page content for hyperlocal trust signals (e.g., "We serve homes in Highland Park, Lakewood, and the M Streets")
9. **Drive time policies** — if the client charges extra for jobs beyond a certain distance, document that policy
10. **Cross-state coverage** if applicable (multi-state operations have additional licensing and citation requirements)

### Why this matters

The platform generates programmatic pages on a per-city, per-service basis. A client claiming "we serve Dallas-Fort Worth" might mean 5 cities or 50 cities — that's a 10× difference in page volume and ranking surface. Granular city lists also enable accurate map embeds, distance calculations, and storm event matching.

Storm corridors are a Phase 1.5 feature but the data must be collected from day one because the operator defines them based on local knowledge that takes weeks to elicit if not captured during onboarding.

### What happens if incomplete

Page generation defaults to the primary city only. Expansion potential blocked. Storm Intelligence Engine cannot filter to relevant geography. Local trust signals on pages are weak.

**Tier:** 1 partial, Tier 2 expansion
**Feeds:** A-02 Page Generator (trust signal modules), A-03 Schema Generator (LocalBusiness credentials), Asset Hub (document upload), A-46 Directory Registration (some directories require certification verification), A-40 External Signal Coordination (manufacturer partner pages)
**Critical for:** Trust signals, conversion rates, premium directory eligibility, manufacturer partnership pages

### Questions

1. **Industry certifications held** (list each with certification number, date earned, expiration date if applicable):
   - IICRC (Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification)
   - NRCA (National Roofing Contractors Association)
   - PDR Nation (for paintless dent repair)
   - NARI (National Association of the Remodeling Industry)
   - HAAG Certified Inspector
   - RCI / IIBEC (Roof Consultants Institute)
   - Other industry-specific certifications
2. **State contractor license numbers** with class designations and expiration dates
3. **BBB accreditation** — accredited or not, rating, year accredited
4. **Insurance and bonding details** — general liability insurance carrier and coverage amount, workers comp coverage, bonding amount
5. **OSHA training certifications** held by the team
6. **Drug testing program** in place (used as trust signal for insurance carrier partnerships)
7. **Background check program** in place for technicians
8. **Awards and recognitions received** (Top Roofer, BBB Torch Award, local business awards, industry publication awards)
9. **Professional designations of key staff** (PE for engineers, CPA if relevant, etc.)
10. **Documents to be uploaded via Asset Hub:**
    - License certificates (PDF)
    - Insurance certificates (PDF)
    - Certification documents (PDF)
    - Award certificates (PDF or images)
    - Bond documents (PDF)

### Why this matters

Trust signals are the highest-converting elements on a page after price and reviews. A page that lists "Licensed, Bonded, Insured, IICRC Certified, BBB A+, GAF Master Elite" converts 2-3× higher than a page without those signals. Several Tier 2 directories (HomeAdvisor Top Rated, Angi Certified, manufacturer-certified directories) require certification verification before approval.

### What happens if incomplete

Pages generated with generic trust language ("licensed and insured") instead of specific certifications. Manufacturer partner page eligibility blocked. Tier 2 directory submissions delayed.

The platform amplifies this differentiation through dedicated pages targeting "[carrier name] approved roofing contractor in [city]" keywords — but only if the carrier relationships are accurately documented.

Several insurance carriers maintain preferred contractor directories that link back to the contractor's site. These are some of the highest-DR backlinks available in the storm restoration vertical. They require verification of preferred status.

### What happens if incomplete

Insurance-specific pages cannot be generated. Carrier directory submissions blocked. Xactimate workflow access limited. Storm-driven content lacks the carrier specificity that converts highest-value leads.

7. **GBP categories currently selected** (primary and secondary)

12. **Current third-party review platforms claimed** (Yelp, Google, BBB, Angi, HomeAdvisor, Houzz, Thumbtack, Nextdoor)

A-21 Client Site Ingestion analyzes the client's existing website to model brand voice. If the existing site is well-written and on-brand, A-02 generates pages that match that voice. If the existing site is generic or unprofessional, A-02 uses default professional contractor voice. Either way, the platform needs the baseline to work from.

A-08 Indexation Tracker establishes the pre-Tarritrix baseline of indexed pages so the platform can measure incremental impact. Without this baseline, the operator can't report "X new pages indexed since onboarding" with credibility.

A-45 Backlink Intelligence runs an existing backlink audit at onboarding to identify what backlinks the client already has — these become trust signals to amplify rather than replace.

### What happens if incomplete

Brand voice modeling defaults to generic. Baseline metrics unknown. Existing backlinks invisible to the platform. Risk of platform overwriting valuable existing content.

## Section 9: Existing Backlinks

**Tier:** 2 (Authority)
**Feeds:** A-45 Backlink Intelligence Engine (initial inventory), A-40 External Signal Coordination (gap analysis), Tier 1-3 backlink campaign prioritization
**Critical for:** Avoiding wasted outreach to sources already linking, identifying toxic links requiring disavow, baseline measurement

### Questions

1. **Known backlinks pointing at the website** — paste URL list or upload CSV (export from Ahrefs, SEMrush, Majestic, or GSC links report)
2. **Press mentions and articles** featuring the business — provide URLs even if they don't link, since A-40 can pursue unlinked mention outreach
3. **Industry publication features** — trade publication articles, podcast appearances, video features
4. **Local news coverage** of the business — even old coverage is valuable for citation outreach
5. **Manufacturer partner page listings** (already partially covered in Section 6 but document the specific URLs here for tracking)
6. **Chamber of commerce member page URLs** (each chamber's member directory is a backlink)
7. **BBB business profile URL** (the BBB profile is a backlink and authority signal)
8. **State / local government contractor registry URLs** (state license boards often have public-facing pages that link to contractor sites)
9. **Insurance carrier preferred contractor directory listings** (Section 5 partially covers this — get the URLs here)
10. **Charity / community involvement page mentions** (Habitat for Humanity contractor lists, etc.)
11. **Any link the client knows about that points to their site** — even social media profile links count for the audit
12. **Suspicious or unknown backlinks** the client wants reviewed for potential disavow

### Why this matters

A-45 needs a baseline inventory to start from. Without it, the agent has to discover every backlink from scratch through third-party API calls, which is expensive and incomplete. Clients often know about backlinks that don't appear in commercial backlink tools (small directories, friend's website mentions, local nonprofit listings) — those are the most valuable to capture during intake.

### What happens if incomplete

A-45 has to do full discovery from scratch. Toxic backlinks may go unidentified for the first 30-60 days. Outreach campaigns may target sources that already link to the site.

## Section 11: Job Photo Library

**Tier:** 2-3 (Authority through Dominance)
**Feeds:** A-22 Job Evidence Ingestion (EXIF validation), A-02 Page Generator (image modules per page), Storm Intelligence Engine (storm event correlation), service area heatmap data
**Critical for:** Page authenticity (real photos vs. stock photos), evidence-tier unlock progression, geographic coverage proof

### Questions

1. **Bulk job photo upload via Asset Hub** — photos with EXIF data preserved (GPS coordinates, timestamps, camera fingerprint). Critical: phone photos transferred via AirDrop, Google Photos, or direct cable preserve EXIF; photos sent via SMS or some messaging apps strip EXIF.
2. **Per-photo metadata required:**
   - Job location (street, city, ZIP)
   - Service performed
   - Date of job
   - Storm event reference if applicable (which storm caused the damage)
   - Before/after pairing (for transformation shots)
   - Customer permission status (do we have permission to use this photo publicly)
3. **Photo categories needed:**
   - Before and after pairs
   - In-progress shots (crew on job, equipment, scaffolding)
   - Damage closeups (hail damage, wind damage, storm damage)
   - Detail shots (specific roofing materials, dent repair detail work)
   - Team and equipment shots (used for "about us" content)
   - Vehicle and signage shots (used for trust signals)
4. **Drone footage** if available (used for premium hero imagery on service hub pages)
5. **Time-lapse video** of complete jobs if available
6. **Customer-permission documentation** — signed release forms or documented consent for each photo's commercial use

### Why this matters

A-22 EXIF validation is one of the platform's strongest authenticity signals. When a city page shows "We've worked in this neighborhood" with photos that have verified GPS coordinates matching that neighborhood, Google and visitors see real proof of local work. Stock photos provide none of this. Photos without EXIF data provide none of this. The intake must capture photo metadata at upload, not after-the-fact.

Photo volume directly drives evidence-tier unlock. Tier 1 requires 10+ verified photos. Tier 2 requires 50+. Tier 3 requires 200+ with geographic distribution across the service area.

### What happens if incomplete

Pages use stock photography (Google detects this and ranks lower). Evidence-tier unlock stalls. Storm event correlations cannot be visually proven.

### Why this matters

Community involvement is the foundation of the "local roots" trust signal that converts homeowners considering between out-of-town storm chasers vs. local established contractors. It's also the foundation of Tier 3 PR campaigns — local news outlets love charity stories from local businesses.

Many nonprofit organizations link from their sponsors page back to sponsor websites. These are legitimate, high-quality backlinks that Google values. The intake captures the relationships so A-45 can pursue confirmation that backlinks exist (or surface opportunity to ask).

### What happens if incomplete

"Local roots" trust signals weak. Community PR angles unavailable. Nonprofit backlink opportunities unidentified.

## Section 15: Sales Process and Lead Routing

**Tier:** 1 (Foundational)
**Feeds:** A-09 Conversion Handler (lead routing), A-19 Universal Integration Hub (CRM integration), Conversion form configuration on every page, Email and SMS notification system
**Critical for:** Lead routing speed (response time is the highest predictor of conversion), TCPA-compliant capture, lead loss prevention

### Questions

1. **Primary lead destination** — where do leads go when they come in (email, SMS, CRM, phone routing)?
2. **Lead routing rules** — different leads to different destinations based on city, service, time of day, urgency level?
3. **Response time SLA** — how quickly does the client commit to responding to a new lead (5 minutes, 15 minutes, 1 hour, 24 hours)?
4. **After-hours lead handling** — does the client have on-call staff, an answering service, or do leads sit until morning?
5. **Sales team structure** — owner-only, dedicated sales team, technician-to-sales handoff?
6. **Lead qualification process** — what makes a lead qualified vs. unqualified for the client's business?
7. **Average lead-to-customer conversion rate** if tracked
8. **Average deal size** by service type
9. **Sales cycle length** from first contact to signed contract
10. **Common objections** customers raise and how the client responds (used to inform FAQ content on pages)
11. **TCPA consent language preferences** — does the client have specific language they require on consent checkboxes?
12. **Emergency vs. routine lead distinction** — does the client want different intake flows for emergency calls vs. estimate requests?

### Why this matters

The conversion form on every Tarritrix-generated page is the primary lead capture mechanism. Its configuration must match the client's sales process exactly. Generic forms convert poorly. Forms that ask the right qualification questions, route to the right destination, and trigger the right response cadence convert dramatically better.

TCPA compliance is non-negotiable. The platform's TCPA immutability system (consent stored with database triggers) requires accurate consent language captured during intake.

### What happens if incomplete

Conversion forms generic. Leads routed incorrectly. TCPA exposure increased. Sales process bottlenecks invisible.

## Section 16: Brand Assets

**Tier:** 1 (Foundational)
**Feeds:** A-02 Page Generator (visual brand consistency), A-21 Client Site Ingestion (brand voice baseline), Asset Hub uploads, every visual element on every page
**Critical for:** Brand consistency across hundreds of generated pages, professional appearance, design system enforcement

### Questions

1. **Logo files in vector format** (SVG, AI, or EPS preferred; PDF acceptable; PNG only as last resort)
   - Primary logo (full color)
   - Reversed logo (white version for dark backgrounds)
   - Icon-only / monogram version
   - Black and white version
2. **Brand color palette** — exact hex codes for:
   - Primary brand color
   - Secondary brand color
   - Accent colors
   - Text colors (body, headlines, links)
3. **Brand fonts** — specify exact font families used in branding:
   - Heading font
   - Body font
   - Display / special font if applicable
   - Confirm font licensing for web use
4. **Brand voice and tone guidelines** — formal, conversational, expert, friendly? Document samples if available.
5. **Existing brand guidelines document** if the client has one — upload via Asset Hub
6. **Photography style preferences** — bright and aspirational vs. documentary and real, color treatment, lighting style
7. **Iconography style** if the brand uses icons (flat, outlined, filled, custom illustrations)
8. **Slogan or tagline** the client uses
9. **Pronunciation guide** for business name if unusual (used for voice search optimization in Phase 1.5)
10. **Trademark status** of business name, logo, and slogan
11. **Brand restrictions** — colors, fonts, words, or imagery the client does NOT want used

### Why this matters

Tarritrix generates hundreds of pages per client. Without consistent brand application, those pages look like a page mill instead of a unified brand experience. Per-client brand signature is one of the platform's penalty-prevention features (every client has a DMA-unique brand signature so generated sites don't share visual fingerprints).

Vector logo files are non-negotiable. PNG logos pixelate when scaled and cannot be color-adjusted for different backgrounds. Many contractors only have PNG files because their original designer never delivered vector files. Part of onboarding is recovering vector files from the original designer or commissioning new ones.

### What happens if incomplete

Pages use generic visual treatment. Brand consistency breaks across page volume. Penalty-prevention fingerprint diversification weakened.
